My Taste

I usually gravitate toward an aesthetic that (or/and)

1. carries a strong hand-drawn or traditional art quality,

2. with a sense of mystery,

3. with a powerful spirituality or emotional intensity,

4. sometimes even tinged with darkness and horror.

Here are some screenshots of my aesthetic collection boards:

From a very young age, my father exposed me to a large number of horror film DVDs—this was probably between the ages of nine and fourteen. He was, in many ways, an irresponsible parent, but looking back, I must have truly enjoyed the experience at the time. That exposure directly shaped my preference for dark aesthetics from adolescence up to the present.

Throughout my growth and studies, I have gone through many traumas. I won’t go into detail here—this isn’t a group therapy session—but these experiences have made me deeply attentive to both my own and others’ inner worlds. Through art, I try to create connections and resonance between these spiritual landscapes.

I believe hand-drawing is the medium closest to our instincts rather than other means like digital drawing or creative coding. It reflects the brain’s impulses in the most direct and immediate way, allowing raw thoughts and emotions to flow on the page.

Therefore, in all my work at ITP—whether in design or in more high tech explorations—I will keep this central theme at the core.

The Boarding Pass

-I reviewed the boarding passes I’ve collected to reflect on my own taste in boarding pass design.

-In Figma, I categorized and prioritized the information.

-I want to design the boarding pass to be very short so it can easily fit into a back pocket. From my experience, I usually keep boarding passes in my back pocket or a jacket pocket, and the ones currently in common use are too long—either they slip out or the exposed half gets worn out, which really annoys me. So I plan to improve this experience in my design. Because of the smaller area, I need to carefully plan the information layout, and I believe a vertical orientation makes it easier to fit in more details.

-The two barcodes need to be separated so they can be scanned easily without accidentally scanning the wrong one.
