Review-99% Invisible, The Secret Life of Color

Source

Roman Mars’ chat with Kassia St. Clair about The Secret Lives of Color starts with this funny little story about kids and their favorite colors, and then suddenly you’re knee-deep in history, science and even gossip about dyes and pigments.

The best part is how smooth the conversation goes. One moment they’re talking about how red shows up everywhere—from prehistoric hematite to expensive scarlet cloth made from crushed bugs—and the next you’re learning about Tyrian purple and how emperors literally punished people for wearing it. And then boom, there’s mauve, a “mistake” that changed fashion forever. Green was deadly (thanks arsenic wallpaper), blue was once considered unlucky before Mary made it holy, and black, well, it’s way more complex than we usually give it credit for.

What I love is how it connects to everyday life. I actually looked at my strawberry yogurt after listening and thought: oh god, am I eating bugs right now? And when I pulled on my favorite blue hoodie, I remembered that once upon a time blue was seen as barbaric. It’s kinda comforting and unsettling at the same time.

Kassia’s stories make you realize people have always gone to ridiculous lengths just to get brighter colors—digging up minerals, crushing insects, even risking poisoning themselves. Humans never really changed, we still chase shiny things.
