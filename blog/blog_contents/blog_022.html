An Example of Synthetic Media

Refik Anadol, Unsupervised

The work takes the form of an AI-driven dynamic video installation that generates ever-changing visual imagery. It utilizes classic art collections—specifically the MoMA collection—as one of its “source material pools” to create new visual content. Anadol incorporates site-specific inputs from the Gund Hall environment—light, movement, acoustics, and external weather variations—to influence the evolving images and sounds.

Why do I consider this synthetic media?

Unlike traditional video art or live recording, Unsupervised exists in constant transformation. The visuals are not filmed; they are produced by an AI model trained on MoMA’s own digital collection. This process fuses historical archives, numerical data, and algorithmic imagination, creating visuals that feel both familiar and entirely alien. In this sense, Unsupervised exemplifies synthetic media: the image is no longer about representation but about generation—a living stream of computation that continuously redefines what “art” looks like.

What can I find about how this was made?

According to Anadol’s team, the project was trained using MoMA’s digitized collection and a mix of visual, auditory, and even climate data to influence the evolving forms.

What are the ethical ramifications of this specific example (if any)?

Copyright and reuse: Using MoMA’s collection as reference material can raise questions about ownership and artistic rights—especially when historical works become raw data.

Originality and authorship: When AI “dreams” from existing artworks, who is the author—the machine, the artist, or the unseen creators of the dataset?

Artistic identity in the age of algorithms: As generative systems like this gain prominence, the role of the human artist shifts—from maker to curator of machine perception.
