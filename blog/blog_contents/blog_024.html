Rough Editing

We roughly edited the general narrative in AE. We employed a straightforward approach to assemble all the assets together, thereby identifying areas requiring adjustment.

Organic Adjustment on Structure

For the sections where AI was originally used but performed poorly, such as the voiceover for some synthetic videos and the object tracking in AE, we intervened organically.

Organic Post-Production

We added timecode, noise, grain, tint, and chromatic aberration to make the texture more close to VHS.

Success and Unsuccess

Success

1. We succeeded in controlling the general style by seeding multiple references.

2. We've successfully cloned the male broadcasting voice from China's old television broadcasts to serve as the narrator's voice.

Unsuccess

1. For most of the specific and detailed demands, Runway cannot understand them correctly.

2. Voices generated together with the video by Runway are unqualified (and take credits).

2. When we attempted to have AI generate uncanny valley portraits based on our hand-drawn sketch, they seemed unable to grasp humans' nuanced reactions to the uncanny valley phenomenon, producing highly industrialized ghost faces instead. Consequently, we had no choice but to organically craft that frame in Photoshop using the protraits found in Google's "uncanny vally police portrait" page.

How would I Approach the Project Differently Knowing What I Know Now?

During the rough editing phase, we discovered that Japanese proved challenging to handle within our linguistic context (Chinese and Korean). We couldn't pinpoint where errors might occur within the film's dense text. So, we shifted the cultural setting to China and reworked all subtitles and narrator voices. This experience serves as a reminder that in future projects, we should strive to avoid cultures unfamiliar to all team members.
