1-Reaction to the COVID Tracking Project Podcast

“We don't have eyes on this virus. You've created holes in our surveillance system.”

This line hit me because it turns "data" from something abstract into something bodily and urgent: eyes. If you don't have eyes on the virus, you're not just missing numbers—you're moving through a disaster half-blind, reacting only after harm has already spread. What's unsettling is that the gap isn't caused by lack of expertise (they clearly had models and experience), but by a system that chose a narrow "flu model" and let reporting become fragmented. It made me think about how a public health failure can start as a visibility failure: when surveillance breaks, accountability breaks with it, and then ordinary people—journalists and volunteers—end up building the "eyes" the government should have had from the start.

2-Data Vizzie One-Climate Spiral

2.1-Why did I Choose the Vizzie?

Because it is a striking and innovative way of showing long-term temperature changes over decades. Unlike a standard line graph, it uses circular motion to visually emphasize how temperature anomalies have spiraled outward over time, making the trend of global warming both engaging and easy to grasp even for non-technical audiences.

2.2-Describe What the Vizzie is Trying to Convey and How

The visualization shows global average temperature anomalies from the mid-19th century to 2018. Each corner represents a month. Each ring represents one year, moving outward as temperatures rise. The color scheme typically goes from cool blues (colder years) to hot reds (warmer years), creating an intuitive visual cue for warming. The spiral design makes it immediately clear that temperatures were fairly stable early on but have accelerated dramatically in recent decades.

2.3-Describe the Facts and Feelings

The visualization is based on real global temperature data and shows a significant increase in average temperatures over time, highlighting the acceleration of global warming especially in the late 20th and early 21st centuries. Visually, the spiraling outward pattern evokes a sense of urgency and escalation. The shift from cooler to hotter colors makes the warming trend feel tangible and alarming.

2.4-What is the source(s) of the data? Is it easy to determine this? Does the source convey integrity to the data?

The original spiral by Ed Hawkins is commonly based on major global surface temperature anomaly datasets.

It’s fairly easy to determine the source because Hawkins’ spiral is widely documented. And it conveys strong integrity.

2.5-Is the data richly segmented? Explain.

Not richly segmented in the visualization: it mainly shows a single global aggregate over time.

2.6-Is the data multivariate? Explain.

The spiral is mostly univariate (temperature anomaly) plotted across time.

2.7-Is it a high volume of data?

It's relatively high data if collected for every month about 168 years.

2.8-Does the data go back in time? How far?

Yes, it goes back to 19th century.

2.9-If relevant, is the data adjusted for consistency?

Yes, these datasets are temperature anomalies calculated relative to a reference period, which improves comparability across time and location.

3-Data Vizzie Two-Eyes on Asteroids

03.01-Why did I Choose the Vizzie?

Because it is a real-time and interactive data visualization tool that helps people explore complex astronomical data, specifically the positions and trajectories of asteroids. It goes beyond static charts and allows users to engage directly with the data, which is a powerful example of how visualization can make abstract science accessible.

3.2-Describe What the Vizzie is Trying to Convey and How

It conveys the current positions and orbits of asteroids in our solar system, updated in real time. Users can rotate the view, zoom in out, and observe asteroid motion over time.

3.3-Describe the Facts and Feelings

The visualization reflects actual astronomical data curated by NASA, showing accurate trajectories and positions of asteroids. It visually represents complex orbital mechanics in an intuitive spatial format. I felt a sense of wonder and fascination when interacting with this visualization. Because you can manipulate the view and time, it invites curiosity and exploration.
