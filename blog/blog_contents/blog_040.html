1-Reaction to the COVID Tracking Project Podcast

What stayed with me most from this episode is the realization that the failure wasn’t simply about missing data, but about a system that was never built to see clearly in a crisis. The U.S. government wasn’t hiding numbers. It just didn’t have them in the first place. That raises an uncomfortable set of questions. Why did a country that prides itself on technological leadership lack a national public-health data infrastructure? Why was responsibility for life-saving information so fragmented that volunteers with spreadsheets became a de facto backbone of federal decision-making? And why, in an emergency measured in hours and days, were rigid bureaucratic rules treated as less negotiable than human lives? What troubles me most is not just that the system broke, but that it broke in ways that seemed predictable. If data determines where ventilators go, who gets protected, and who survives, why wasn’t data treated as critical infrastructure long before the pandemic?

And I need to be open-minded more honest to the objective data and facts.
