1-World Background

Following the advice from the previous class, I based my world background settings on an existing game world—Outlast. Throughout the series, the world setting remains consistent: The Murkoff Corporation, an American transnational company, conducts human experiments in forgotten locations, attempting to create artificial nanospirits for warfare.

Typically, each game in the series takes place in a specific location. For instance, Outlast occurred at Mount Massive Asylum, while Outlast II unfolded in Temple Gate, a town deep in the wilderness and hidden from civilization. Therefore, if I were to expand upon this foundation with new concepts, I should create a new region within this premise.

After research, I decided to set the stage in a federal maximum security prison nestled within snow-capped mountains named Whiteridge Federal Penitentiary. Located in the northernmost mountain range of the United States, this facility was converted from abandoned mineshafts. Perennially blanketed in snow and battered by relentless blizzards, it keeps the nation's most violent and depraved criminals. It is considered one of the world's most escape-proof prisons. Murkoff bribed government officials to establish a laboratory within this prison, conducting human experiments on inmates in an attempt to fuse human individuals into fungal-like units possessing collective intelligence network, for use in military reconnaissance and infiltration (Project Red Vein).

The core concept begins with a paradox of human intelligence. Human beings possess one of the most powerful computational systems in the biological world: the brain. Yet the fundamental inaccessibility of individual minds prevents humanity from achieving perfect cooperation. Intelligence remains fragmented, isolated within bodies. In nature, however, fungi have accomplished what humans cannot. Through fusion and continuous connection, fungal organisms form vast mycelial networks—distributed systems of collective intelligence that can extend across entire forests. These networks do not rely on centralized control, but on constant communication, adaptation, and shared response. What if the human brain—already so computationally advanced—could be fused and linked in the same way? What kind of intelligence would emerge if human cognition were no longer confined to the individual? Murkoff initiated Project Red Vein to explore this possibility. The project seeks to investigate the formation of a human collective intelligence network, and to evaluate its potential for large-scale military application.

Searching across major platforms, I confirmed that the human collective intellegence network and the prison in a snowy mountain are relatively innovative concepts.

2-Gameplay

The gameplay will be almost the same as Outlast. It is expected to be a survival horror game. In order to survive, players must either run or hide from enemies. Combat is not possible and attempts to confront an enemy will result in death. The only item players will have equipped is a camcorder. The camcorder is battery operated and includes night vision, which is used to navigate through dark areas. Batteries need to be collected throughout the game to keep the camcorder's night vision working. The story unravels through documents that can be found in various locations.

Due to the region's extreme cold, players must maintain body temperature while moving outdoors. The protagonist has a body temperature limit; once the temp value drops to zero, they will die from hypothermia. Therefore, in addition to batteries for the camcorder, players must also collect batteries for the cold-prove clothes.

3-Storytelling

The protagonist is a data scientist working for the government. She was framed and sent to the Whiteridge Federal Penitentiary after uncovering the conspiracy between the government and Murkoff.
