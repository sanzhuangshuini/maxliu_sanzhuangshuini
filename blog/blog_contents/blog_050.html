2-Title

Does Crowding Cause Chaos? Visualizing the Relationship Between Ridership and Delay Rates Across NYC Subway Lines

3-Brief Description

Using monthly ridership and delay rate data from 24 subway lines over a full calendar year, this project explores whether higher ridership and accident rates are associated with higher delay rates.

4-Why This Topic

Public transit reliability is one of the most tangible ways city infrastructure affects daily life. Delays are frustrating, and it's natural to assume that crowded trains are delayed trains — but is that actually true? We were drawn to this question because it sits at the intersection of urban data and public perception. If the busiest lines aren't the worst performers, that reframes how we think about transit investment and accountability.

7-Questions Remaining & Next Steps

After examining ridership and accident rates, we found neither showed a particularly strong single-factor correlation with delay rates. We hypothesize that delays on the New York subway represent a highly multifaceted and complex issue, potentially influenced by factors such as the age of the lines, operating companies, and the independence of individual routes. In next steps, we plan to conduct further in-depth investigations to arrive at systematic conclusions explaining subway delays in New York.
