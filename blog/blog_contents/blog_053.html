Ideation

What if there is no causality at all?
What if everything happens in true randomness?
What if the residents do not make any decisions? They just live, exist, and die, maybe feel.
The only thing that is differentiating the residents and the environment around them is their cognitive or awareness of the existence or boundary of themselves.
What if their lifespan is extremely short comparing to us humans in this world? One minute? One second? One millisecond?
Causality is a weapon of us to fight time. We use it to conclude, to decide, and to predict.
But if a creature live so shortly, they do not need causality.

Human faith is almost always a response to uncertainty. I don' t know what tomorrow will bring, and I don' t know where I' ll go after death, so I need a framework to contain this unease.
But these residents don' t grapple with the question of “what tomorrow will be.” Their lifespans are so short that death is not a distant shadow. It' s simply part of what’ s happening right now, just like breathing.
Uncertainty is simply not an issue for them.
So what, then, is left of faith?
I think they will develop a faith of collective connection to counteract the inevitable loneliness. Since each individual' s life and experiences are entirely random, the overlap in their collective perceptions of one another will be exceptionally small. Every single one of them experiences profound loneliness.
They have faith in the collective, yet the collective remains almost untouchable to them. It' s as if we have faith in eternity, yet we are mortal.

The machine in our world is practically the direct daughter of science, which is the best student of causality.
In a world without causality, the machine will not be just “more advanced” or “more underdeveloped” than ours, but belonging to a brand new system.
NO!
NO!
NO!
There will be NO machine in their world!
Someday, a MACHINE arrived out of nowhere without a knowing reason, a MACHINE from the outer space, from a parallel universe, maybe from the true randomness!  
True randomness has produced the one thing that is not random—which is a paradox in itself, but paradoxes are precisely the most mythical origins.
Moreover, this logic speaks for itself mathematically. Over a sufficiently long period of time, true randomness will exhaust all possibilities, including the possibility of “producing a regular structure.” Just as a thousand monkeys typing on typewriters could, in theory, produce a work by Shakespeare.
Will this MACHINE naturally become GOD in this world?

I am considering set the world to be a micro world.
