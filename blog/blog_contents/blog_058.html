1-What is my topic and concept??

What role does movement play in your own life or communities that you are part of?

I have anxiety and bipolar disorder, which makes me interested in how our mental states affect the way we move. Anxiety or emotional stress can appear through small movements, such as changes in posture, pacing, fidgeting, or becoming physically still.

What are the historical, social, or cultural contexts of your chosen movement?

From a social and cultural perspective, movement does not always have a fixed meaning. In Dictionary of Gestures, François Caradec shows that the same gesture can carry very different meanings across cultures, suggesting that movement is interpreted through social and cultural context rather than understood by movement alone. A movement caused by anxiety or a change in mood may feel natural or self-regulating to the person experiencing it, while an outside observer may interpret it as nervousness, impatience, or unusual behavior. The gap between inner experience and social interpretation of movement would make a worthy concept.

Another source: Hinshaw & Stier (2008), Stigma as Related to Mental Disorders

Which ethnographic methods, if any, might be relevant to your research and why?

Both autoethnography and participatory research could be relevant to this project. I can document and reflect on my own lived experience, while also arranging collective interview and movement activities.

How might this practice be expressed through motion capture? What challenges do you foresee?

Mocap can simulate the extraction of movements within a social context. When a movement is separated from the subject through objective technical means, how will it be perceived? At the same time, I want to avoid suggesting that a specific movement can directly represent or diagnose a particular mental condition.

What permissions and resources do you need to respectfully engage with your subject?

Informed consent, privacy, and participant control are especially important. Participants should be able to decide what experiences they want to share, what movements can be recorded, and whether their motion capture data can be shown publicly.
