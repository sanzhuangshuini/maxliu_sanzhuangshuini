1-What is my topic and concept?

1.1-What idea or issue will you be tackling?

I want to make a narrative game about my original characters: a small group of people whose lives are tangled together through crime and romance.

1.2-What are some of the questions that drive your interest?

I'm curious whether a game can feel emotionally rich without a complicated plot, if the story stays simple but strange things happen visually and tonally. And I want to explore identity that isn't easy to pin down, gender, sanity, and mentality, and see what happens when the player can't fully trust what they're looking at.

2-Think about the constraints that can help guide your creative process

Time is the biggest one. This is my graduation thesis, so I have a fixed number of months to get something playable, which means I probably can't build the whole story I imagine. I think I'll need to scope this down to a short prelude, maybe 2-3 hours of playtime.

I'm also a one-person-team, so the "innovative interactions" I want can't be too complex to build.

There's also a tool constraint. I would like to limit the game in Unity, which means I can't use any fancy UE functions.

And since this is a thesis project that I might want to show publicly or even publish later, I need to think about how dark or disturbing the content can get — some of the themes I'm drawn to (violence, mental illness, disturbing imagery) might need boundaries depending on where and how the game gets shown.
