1-Review on Notes on Blindness

The thing about the piece is that it doesn't simply try to simulate the visual of the blindness. Instead, it lets us experience the world and the sensing logic of the visually impaired—sounds make objects appear visually. The interaction is limited and simple, but works very well with the storytelling. Despite the limitation, the piece still provides a unique engagement. I especially like the second chapter, Feeling the Wind. Instead of just listening to John Hull describe the wind, we can actually create the wind and use it to make objects move, make sounds, and become visible. Overall, it feels more like experiencing someone else's way of perceiving the world rather than just watching a stroy about blindness.

2-Review on Pearl

I think the way the piece tells its story—using an old car as the thread that connects the whole film—works especially well in VR. You can tell that the creators really considered VR as part of the storytelling from the beginning. The audience doesn't need to move around; we can simply sit in the passenger seat and turn our heads while the characters perform around us. This gives us a strong sense of space and presence without using movement-based interactions that could feel overwhelming or cause motion sickness. At the same time, it makes the story very easy to follow from beginning to end.
