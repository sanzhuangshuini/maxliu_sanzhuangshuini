1-With $10,000 and 10 Months

Ten months is ample time for me to craft this "Prelude" according to my vision, and the $10,000 budget is very generous.

Fund allocation: Primarily for outsourcing specialized tasks I cannot handle alone, music, and marketing/promotion—such as character rigging and animation support; hiring a composer for key emotional moments; purchasing assets from stores (like basic environmental components) to modify rather than building from scratch; collaborating with influencers to promote the game, and so on.

Time allocation: First, I will fully construct the main storyline—this forms the backbone of the Prelude, while all other content remains optional. Ten months provides a comfortable margin; once the main storyline is complete, there is significant scope for developing branching content.

2-With $10 and 10 Days

"Ten dollars and ten days" wasn't just a scaled-down version of the original plan; it posed a fundamentally different question: what is the minimal vehicle required to prove this concept works?

The answer wasn't a full game, but a single scene. Specifically, the opening: cash, sunglasses, a crowd, voiceover monologue of Persia, who is our protagonist, and then—without warning—a sudden cut into her nightmare. Those brief 90 seconds distilled the entire essence of *Never Been Easier*—decay lurking beneath a glamorous surface—while also signaling that the game wouldn't be holding the player's hand.

Where would that ten dollars go? Almost nowhere. I'd use only free tools. That ten dollars might just cover a font license, a pre-made sound effect pack... or just a drink for myself.

And the ten days? No complex systems, no branching narratives, no UI polishing. Just one scene: the crowd shots, the monologue, and the abrupt transition into the nightmare. Even using greybox—like solid color blocks or simple shapes to represent characters—would be fine, because the focus wasn't on visual fidelity but on pacing: would that jarring emotional contrast actually work? When a stranger experienced it for the first time, would they feel that shock and sense of weightlessness—like the ground suddenly giving way beneath their feet—just as I intended?
