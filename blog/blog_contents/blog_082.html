3-Playtest Results

3.1-General Feedback

The experience is highly immersive.

Most players came away with a clear picture of the protagonist, but not of the story.

They read her as smart but a bit of a player, and possibly going through a low point or some depression, because her room is such a mess.

The narrative itself is still vague to them. Some guessed it's about intimate relationships; others compared it to a film filed under "Drama", with no specific theme they could name.

3.2-What Worked

The protagonist has personality. Players pieced her character together from the space and the objects alone, and their reading of her was consistent from person to person.

The messy room does a lot of the storytelling. Nobody was told she might be struggling; they inferred it from the environment.

The basic gameplay is clear.

3.2-What Didn't

The robot placeholder model is very confusing. Without any hint, most players assumed this is a story about robots. Interestingly, a few said it would be fun if the protagonist really were a robot.

The narrative isn't clear yet. Players understood who she is, but not what the story is about.

The space doesn't read as an RV: it's far too roomy. This was intentional, since I wanted players to have room to learn how to walk, but nobody recognized it as an RV. It may also be because the level is still a draft.

4-Next Steps

The robot placeholder turned out to affect how players understand the game much more than I expected, so replacing it with the real human model moves up my list. Until then, I'll tell players up front that the robot is a placeholder.

I'll make the narrative more specific by adding mechanics to the scene, so players leave knowing what the story is about, not only who the protagonist is.

In the refined version I'll make it clear that the space is an RV, while keeping enough room for players to learn how to walk.

I'll keep what works: the environmental storytelling, and the protagonist's personality coming through the room.
