02IdeaGame Concept
Two players, two scales

Game
Concept

This is a two-player cooperative game set in a children's playroom. One player builds within a VR environment, while the other plays a platformer on PC.

VR

The VR player views the room from a first-person perspective, physically placing building blocks and interacting with doors and windows in the environment.

PC

Meanwhile, the PC player controls a small character from a third-person perspective, using the blocks as platforms and navigating through wind currents generated by wind blowing in through the windows to reach the objective.

02 — Hearing

How do sounds feel real?

1. Driven by physics, not scripts

OnComponentHit fires whenever an object actually collides. Sound happens at the exact moment of contact, never on a timer.

2. Strength → Volume

Impact = impulse × mass, so the same rule works for every object size. A gentle tap plays at 35% volume, a hard slam at 100%.

3. Size → Pitch

Each object's volume (scale X × Y × Z) maps to pitch. Small blocks sound high and bright; big objects sound low and heavy.

4. Material → Tone

Per-object settings: balls use a lower pitch plus a low-pass filter for a soft, muffled thud. Block-on-block hits rise in pitch to sound like wood knocking wood.

5. Rolling follows speed

Every frame (Tick), the blueprint reads the object's velocity and ground contact. Faster rolling is louder and slightly higher, and the sound fades out smoothly when the object stops.

6. Sound lives in 3D space

Every sound is played at the object, with distance attenuation. In VR, players hear where things are as they turn their heads.

7. Filtering out the noise

A 0.08 s cooldown stops duplicate hits, resting objects never trigger sounds, and continuous collision detection (CCD) keeps fast objects from clipping through the floor and buzzing.
