Crowding is not the primary cause of chaos. There is no meaningful positive correlation between ridership and delay rate across the system. The busiest lines, 6 and 1, maintain moderate to low delay rates year-round, while B, 2, and F carry mid-level ridership yet rank among the most delayed. For commuters weighing parallel routes, the chart offers a clear signal: lines closer to the bottom-right are the better bet, offering higher frequency with lower disruption.

Delay rate shows no positive correlation with incident rate per million riders either. N, G, and 2 carry the highest incident rates in the system, yet none of them lead the delay rankings. B sits near the bottom for incident rate at 16th out of 20, yet tops the delay chart. The evidence points elsewhere: delay is likely shaped by a combination of shared track infrastructure, route complexity, and operational redundancy rather than any single measurable cause.
